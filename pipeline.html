From the PDE to the linear solver: the LVPP pipeline, end to end

Companion to lvpp/report.html (findings) and lvpp/RESEARCH.md (lab notebook). This document is the pedagogical one: it walks the full path from the constrained PDE in the LVPP paper to the finite-element discretization we run to the exact linear-solver configuration that survived testing. Every measured claim carries its number.

Step 0 The problem: a variational inequality dressed as a PDE

Model problem (the sphere benchmark): find \(u\) on \(\Omega = (0,2)^2\) (with prescribed boundary data \(u = u_D\)) such that

$$ -\Delta u = f \quad \text{in } \Omega, \qquad u \ge \psi \ \text{ a.e. in } \Omega . $$

This is a variational inequality: an elliptic PDE plus a pointwise inequality constraint. The classical strong form carries a Lagrange-type multiplier \(\Lambda \le 0\) supported on the contact set:

$$ -\Delta u - f = \Lambda, \qquad \Lambda \le 0, \qquad \Lambda\,(u - \psi_{\mathrm{obs}}) = 0, \qquad u \ge \psi_{\mathrm{obs}} . $$

Complementarity — the product \(\Lambda (u-\psi_{\mathrm{obs}}) = 0\) — is what makes this hard: it is a nonsmooth, combinatorial object. Classical solvers resolve it by deciding, each Newton iteration, which nodes are active (vinewtonrsls: active rows are replaced by the constraint equation — the operator's structure mutates). That mutation is what a matrix-free/QFunction library cannot express. LVPP's wager: reparametrize the constraint as a smooth map so the operator never changes.

For generality it matters that the LVPP formulation does not require an energy: the general form is a constrained nonlinear PDE system

$$ \mathcal{F}(u) = 0 \ \text{ in } \Omega, \qquad c(u) \ge 0 \ \text{ pointwise}, $$

with \(c\) handled through a Legendre duality pair (a "Legendre function" \(\mathcal{R}\) whose gradient map \(\nabla\mathcal{R}^{*}\) parametrizes the feasible set). Our implementation keeps this surface: lvpp/legendre.py holds the families (ShannonLower/Upper, FermiDirac, Hellinger, GibbsSimplex) and constraints.py exposes the family-generic interface (coupling_form, state_form, observable) — the same surface the saddle estimator (Finding 3) is built on.

Step 1 The latent-variable saddle system (continuous)

For the unilateral lower bound \(u \ge \psi_{\mathrm{obs}}\), the Shannon-type family gives the exponential slack: the gap is written as

$$ g := u - \psi_{\mathrm{obs}} = e^{\phi}, \qquad \phi \in \mathbb{R}\ \text{ unconstrained} . $$

Feasibility for any \(\phi\), by construction. The inequality is gone; what remains is a saddle-point system in \((u, \phi)\). Because the raw system inherits the complementarity kink, LVPP solves a sequence of proximal subproblems: given the previous latent iterate \(\phi_k\) and parameter \(\alpha_k\), find \((u_{k+1}, \phi_{k+1})\) such that

$$ \begin{aligned} \text{(momentum row)} &\qquad \mathcal{A}(u) - f + \Lambda(\phi) = 0,\\ \text{(constraint row)} &\qquad M\,(u - \psi_{\mathrm{obs}}) - M\,e^{\phi} + \text{prox}_{\alpha_k}(\phi;\phi_k) = 0 . \end{aligned} $$

The two-row structure is the paper's eq. (2.7) in its discrete-friendly form: a PDE row carrying the contact force \(\Lambda\) (which converges to the physical multiplier — the "drift" we measure is exactly \(\lambda_k = (\phi_{k-1}-\phi_k)/\alpha_k\), [PG] Thm 4.13), and a constraint row that substitutes the exponential slack and adds the Bregman proximal term.

The α-sequence is the double-exponential rule of the reference implementation (C = 1, r = 1.5, q = 1.5, capped at α_max = 10): α doubles per proximal step early on, and the number of proximal iterations is mesh-independent (measured 8/11/8/8/8 — Newton totals 21/23/19/20/21).

Step 2 Finite-element discretization

Both unknowns use equal-order continuous piecewise-linear elements on the same mesh — the proximal-Galerkin choice:

$$ V_h = W_h = \{ \text{P1 Lagrange on } \mathcal{T}_h \}, \qquad z = (u_h, \phi_h) \in V_h \times W_h . $$

Everything is assembled through the family-generic residual interface. In our lvpp implementation the two rows are, symbolically,

row 1 (u):   ∫ ∇u·∇v dx  −  ∫ Λ(φ) v dx      =  ∫ f v dx     (momentum + contact force)
row 2 (φ):   ∫ (u − ψ_obs − e^φ) w dx  +  prox(φ; φ_k, α_k) = 0     (constraint + regularization)

where \(\Lambda(\phi)\) is delivered by the Legendre dual of the chosen family — for ShannonLower (the lower-bound family used on the sphere) the dual map produces the \(e^{\phi}\)-weighted terms in both rows. Linearizing gives the block Jacobian

$$ J \;=\; \begin{bmatrix} K & B \\[2pt] M & D \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \begin{aligned} K &= \alpha\,(\text{P1 stiffness}) + \text{(proximal terms on } u\text{)} &&\text{SPD}\\ B &= M &&\text{mass coupling}\\ D &= -\,M \odot e^{\phi_h} + \partial_\phi\,\text{prox} &&\text{scaled mass — degenerate} \end{aligned} $$

and this is where the continuous trick leaves its mark: differentiating \(e^{\phi_h}\) brings the factor \(e^{\phi_h}\) into the latent block, so wherever the gap \(u - \psi_{\mathrm{obs}}\) closes, the diagonal of \(D\) decays like \(e^{\phi}\). The proximal term adds an \(O(1/\alpha_k)\)-scaled healthy bulk on top. Measured at a converged deep state (L2): diagonal entries of \(D\) span \(10^{-28}\)…\(10^{-2}\); 749 rows are below \(10^{-12}\).

Step 2 The nonlinear outer loop: proximal point + Newton

The proximal loop is nonnegotiable structure: α_k grows (double-exponential rule, α_max = 10), each subproblem is solved by Newton, and the discrete multiplier/drift \(\lambda = (\phi_{k-1} - \phi_k)/\alpha_k\) converges to the contact force ([PG] Thm 4.13). Measured through L4: proximal iterations 8/11/8/8/8, Newton 21/23/19/20/21 — mesh-independent, and identical to assembled-LU references. Each Newton step needs one linear solve with J above; this is where all the difficulty lives.

Operator design rule (measured, Finding 1): the system rows never mutate — the fixed-pattern matrix is exactly the libCEED compatibility argument. And the proximal dynamics require exact Newton steps: regularizing the true operator J (ψ_floor_operator) fails at every level, while preconditioner-side modifications leave the proximal map intact.

Step 3 Inside Newton: outer GMRES on a matfree operator

Each Newton linear solve is a Krylov solve with a matrix-free operator: the matfree action of J applies pointwise kernels (e^φ, e^{2φ}, (λ/α)e^φ factors) with fixed sparsity — no row mutation, ever. Verified bit-exact against assembled references through the full loop (Finding 1: 8/21, 11/23 at L0/L1 iteratively identical). The preconditioner, however, is built from the assembled Pmat (firedrake assembles Jp; the matfree operator cannot be block-split).

Why ψ_floor = 0

The floor (ε·mass on the latent block of the Pmat) was our stabilization for an earlier additive configuration whose latent solve divided by machine-zero. The decomposition experiments isolated its cost precisely: an exact block-LU PC with unfloored S⁻¹ gives outer GMRES its = 1 flat at every depth, while the floored variant reproduces the whole growth curve (145/895/1000-diverged) — the ε·mass differs from the operator by O(ε) on a subspace of growing dimension. On the Schur path the latent block is never inverted: Sp = D − M·diag(K)⁻¹·Mᵀ is nonsingular without any floor (the coupling carries the dead rows). End-to-end ψ_floor = 0 is LU-identical with max outer its 14/29/48 vs 45/130–560/400–926 floored. The floor remains necessary only on additive/diagonal- inversion paths; the ε-ladder maps its safety band (≤ 1e-5 safe, 1e-4 knife-edge, ≥ 1e-3 kills L2).

Step 4 The Schur complement: why this configuration works

Block elimination of the healthy u-block leaves the latent Schur complement

$$ S \;=\; D - M^{\top}K^{-1}M , $$

and the measured spectral anatomy is the heart of the whole story: S never degenerates. On the contact interior D contributes ~0, and S there is carried entirely by the coupling \(-M^{\top}K^{-1}M\) — the smoothing operator, which stays strictly negative definite with \(\lambda_{S,\min} \sim -c\,h^{2}\) (pure h² scaling, α-independent, no near-null cluster: 0/1/1 across levels). The dead latent rows of J are numerically harmless to a solver that approximates S through the coupling — they are lethal only to a preconditioner that inverts D directly (which is why additive fieldsplit needed the floor and Schur does not).

Step 4b Graded meshes: the matfree S-action variant

On adaptive (band) meshes the selfp approximation degrades (outer its up to 323, and a DIVERGED_BREAKDOWN at 14k dofs). The fix keeps the same block-UL structure but replaces the Sp solve by the true Schur action applied matrix-free via the triple product

$$ S\,v \;=\; D\,v \;-\; B^{\top}\big(K^{-1}(B\,v)\big), $$

with each K⁻¹ application an exact MUMPS solve, and the (now variable-iteration) Schur solve done by a capped inner Krylov method on \(-S\) (rtol 1e-4, cap 40, Jacobi-scaled; outer FGMRES because the Schur apply is now iteration-varying). End-to-end on the band chain:

  • band_l4 (3613 dofs, grading 22.8×): max outer its 161 vs 297 (selfp); deep proximal steps 297 → 4–5 its.
  • band_l5 (45.6×): solves 8/17, err 4.995e-03, max 188 vs 323 (selfp).
  • band_l6 (14,245 dofs): every linear solve CONVERGED_RTOL where selfp production diverged (ksp = −5) — the graded-mesh failure is eliminated.

Costs, measured honestly: on uniform meshes variant B is ~4.5× slower than selfp (the first, coldest Newton solve pays 62–70 its) — its value is grading-robustness, not uniform speed. And with inner CG, one stall was observed (band_l5 proximal increment plateau with all linear solves at 4–5 its): the capped inexact Schur action perturbs the Newton matrix within tolerance; the identical chain with inner GMRES solves band_l5 in 8 prox steps. The S-action assumes no symmetry, so for non-potential families (convection, q-Laplacian) the same structure holds with the inner method's name changed from CG to GMRES.

Family generality. The matfree triple product assumes no symmetry. For the symmetric/energy case −S is SPD (strictly negative S, h²-scaled) and CG is the efficient inner choice; for non-potential \(\mathcal{F}\) (convection, q-Laplacian), S becomes nonsymmetric and the inner method becomes GMRES/MINRES with the same tolerance/cap discipline. The architecture — fixed-pattern operator, Schur-block treatment, proximal outer loop — is unchanged; only the inner method's name and the loss of the spectral bound are family-dependent.
